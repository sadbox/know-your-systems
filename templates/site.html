Paper and tools

Cover
67 lb vellum bristol cover stock in light orange, printed in black only. It is stiff enough to protect the pages and still folds cleanly once scored.
Inside pages
28 lb bright white letter paper, printed in full color. We use Staples 28 lb: it is acid-free, so it lasts in a drawer for years; heavy, so ink doesn’t show through; bright; and it has a little tooth, so pencil writes well on it.
Supply list
Matte letter laminating pouches (9 × 11½ in), not glossy: matte takes a wet-erase marker better and doesn’t glare.
Tools
A creaser or scoring board for the cover, and a saddle stapler to staple through the fold (ours is the Bostitch booklet stapler, B440SB, good for up to 20 sheets; a booklet is 7), and a guillotine paper cutter to trim the booklet’s open edge. For the supply list, a grommet punch and grommets, and metal rings to hold the two sheets together.

Make a booklet

  1. Print the cover on the cover stock in black: landscape, two-sided, flip on short edge, actual size (never “fit to page”).
  2. Print the interior on the 28 lb paper in full color, the same way. That makes six sheets.
  3. Score the cover down its middle with the creaser, then fold it on the score. Cover stock folded without a score cracks along the fold.
  4. Stack the six inside sheets in the order they printed (page 24 | 1 on the outside, 12 | 13 in the middle), fold them together, and nest them in the cover.
  5. Staple twice through the fold. Open the folded booklet a little and drape it over the stapler’s slanted saddle, outside up, with the fold under the stapling head. Put in two staples, each about 2 in from the top or bottom edge; their legs close on the middle pages.
  6. Trim the open edge. Once it’s folded, the inside sheets stick out a little past the cover at the open edge, the middle ones most. Lay the closed booklet in the guillotine with the fold against the back fence, clamp it, and cut the open edge square, taking off no more than ⅛ in. Every page has more than 0.4 in of margin on that edge, so the trim cuts nothing printed.

All content sits at least 0.3 in from the sheet edges, so an ordinary office printer won’t clip it. No cover stock? Print the booklet on plain paper from one file (below).

Make the laminated supply list

  1. Print the supply list on white paper: portrait, two-sided, flip on long edge, actual size. Pages 1 and 2 make sheet 1; pages 3 and 4 make sheet 2.
  2. Laminate each sheet in a pouch. Nothing needs trimming.
  3. Punch a grommet through the clear border in the top-left corner of each sheet, and put the two sheets on a metal ring.
  4. Hand them out with the booklet and a wet-erase marker. People tick an item and write the date they bought it, and wipe it clean with a damp cloth when they replace it.
