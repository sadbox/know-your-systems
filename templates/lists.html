{{/* The lists. Edit an item here and every place that prints it follows. The check-up lists (li-...) print in the booklet: "seasons", "storm" and "warm" in interior.html. The supply lists (sup-...) print on the laminated supply list (sheet.html) and nowhere else. In a supply item, the is the item and the optional is how long it keeps. */}} {{define "li-every-fall"}}
  • Show everyone how to run the generator and transfer switch safely, and where the tools and the outage bucket are
  • Check the dates on stored food, water, fuel, medicine and batteries. Eat what is close to its date, and replace it.
  • Chimneys cleaned and inspected
  • Generator: change the oil, then run it until it is warm
  • Winter tires on, tread checked
  • Pipes insulated; outside faucets shut off and drained
  • Get sand for the driveway. Most town garages have a pile; bring a bucket and shovel.
  • Check the winter supplies: shovel, roof rake, salt, handwarmers
  • Walk through the workbook again
    • {{end}} {{define "li-all-year"}}
  • Keep the drainage around the house clear and in repair
  • Deal with trees that could fall on the house, the lines or the road
  • Refill vehicles and fuel tanks at half, not at empty. You can’t always get more.
  • Test smoke and CO alarms
  • Talk with neighbors about how you would help each other
    • {{end}} {{define "li-storm-coming"}}
  • Watch the forecast and your alerts
  • Gas up the vehicles, fill the fuel cans, check the grill’s propane tank
  • Order fuel if the tank is under half
  • Do the dishes and laundry, bathe, fill the tub
  • Fill a thermos with hot water
  • Lay out headlamps, solar lights and candles
  • Charge phones and power banks
  • Run the generator to check it
  • Know where the shelter is. If you need care such as dialysis, go early.
    • {{end}} {{define "li-power-out"}}
  • Tape the refrigerator and freezer shut
  • Stay away from downed lines
  • Report the outage to your utility
  • Switch off electric heaters, the range and the water heater so they don’t all restart at once
  • Move into your warm room and seal off the rest
  • Check on your neighbors
    • {{end}} {{define "li-deep-winter"}}
  • Dress in layers. Take one off before you sweat; add one before you chill.
  • Shovel as the snow falls. Don’t let it pile up, melt and refreeze.
  • Sand icy steps and walks
  • Rake heavy snow off the roof from the ground, never alone
  • Keep furnace, heater and dryer vents clear
  • Store food outside, below 32°F, where animals can’t get it
  • Trickle the hot and cold taps and open sink cabinets
  • If the cold goes on with no heat, shut off the water main and drain the pipes
    • {{end}} {{define "li-comes-back"}}
  • Throw out food that warmed up
  • Restock batteries, fuel and supplies
  • Note what you would do differently
    • {{end}} {{define "sup-water"}}
  • Drinking water: 1 to 2 gallons a person a day, for two weeks store-bought: date on the bottle; home-filled: 6 months
  • Food-grade jugs or containers to fill
  • Plain unscented bleach, a filter or tablets to treat water bleach: 1 year
  • Buckets for hauling and flushing
  • A thermos, to fill with hot water before a storm
    • {{end}} {{define "sup-food"}}
  • Canned meat, fish, beans, vegetables and soup 2 to 5 years
  • Canned fruit and tomatoes 12 to 18 months
  • Pasta and white rice about 2 years
  • Dried beans, oatmeal, cereal about 1 year
  • Peanut butter, nuts, crackers 6 to 12 months
  • Energy bars, dried fruit, jerky date on the pack
  • Shelf-stable or powdered milk date on the pack
  • Home-canned food 1 year
  • Freeze-dried emergency meals, if rotating food is a chore sold with a 25-year shelf life; they need water
  • Baby food and formula, special diets use-by date
  • Pet food date on the bag
  • Coffee, tea, salt, sugar, oil, spices, treats
  • Manual can opener, paper plates and cups
    • {{end}} {{define "sup-light"}}
  • Flashlights, headlamps, solar lights: one for each person
  • Extra AA, AAA, D and 9-volt batteries up to 10 years; go by the date on the pack
  • Battery or hand-crank radio
  • Power bank and phone cords top up every few months
  • Candles in safe holders, lighters, matches in a waterproof box
  • Heavy outdoor extension cords, where everyone can find them
    • {{end}} {{define "sup-warm"}}
  • A wool blanket or sleeping bag for each person
  • Long layers, hats, sturdy boots
  • Chemical handwarmers date on the pack
  • Plastic sheeting, bubble wrap, duct tape and zip ties to seal off rooms
  • Snow shovel, roof rake, snowshoes, sand and salt
    • {{end}} {{define "sup-tools"}}
  • A 5-gallon bucket with a screw-on lid, for the outage kit
  • Fire extinguisher check the gauge; date on the label
  • Smoke alarms 10 years from the date on the back
  • CO alarms 5 to 7 years
  • Refrigerator and freezer thermometers
  • Wrench or pliers to shut off utilities
  • Work gloves, dust masks, tarp, rope
  • Whistle, paper, pencils, a marker for dating supplies
    • {{end}} {{define "sup-fuel"}}
  • Gasoline in approved cans, kept outside the house 3 to 6 months; about a year with stabilizer
  • Fuel stabilizer
  • Propane cylinders, full, stored outdoors and away from the house does not go stale
  • Generator oil, air filter, spark plug
  • Camp stove or grill and its fuel, for outdoors only
    • {{end}} {{define "sup-med"}}
  • Prescription medicines: an extra supply date on the label; use the oldest first
  • A written list of medicines, doses and doctors, and copies of prescriptions
  • Pain and fever reducer, allergy medicine, antacid, anti-diarrheal date on the label
  • Stop the Bleed kit check dates each fall
  • Nitrile gloves, gauze pads and rolls, bandages, tape sterile gauze: 3 to 5 years
  • Elastic wraps, a moldable splint, a blister kit
  • Instant cold packs, reusable heat packs date on the pack
  • Tweezers, shears, thermometer, emergency blankets
  • Spare glasses, hearing-aid batteries
    • {{end}} {{define "sup-hygiene"}}
  • Toilet paper, moist wipes, hand sanitizer check the sanitizer’s date
  • Personal and feminine hygiene supplies
  • Diapers and wipes, if you need them
  • Heavy trash bags, and cat litter or sawdust for a bucket toilet
    • {{end}} {{define "sup-papers"}}
  • Cash in small bills, $200 or more
  • IDs, Social Security cards, birth certificates, passports, insurance and bank papers and family photos, in a waterproof container you can carry
    • {{end}} {{define "sup-kids"}}
  • Games, books, puzzles, comfort items
    • {{end}} {{define "sup-pets"}}
  • Water and bowls
  • Leash, crate and a photo of each
  • Waste bags or litter, and something familiar
  • Extra feed for livestock
    • {{end}} {{define "sup-vehicle"}}
  • Emergency kit in the car: jump pack, small shovel, cat litter, gloves
